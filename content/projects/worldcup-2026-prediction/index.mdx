## The problem

Predicting a World Cup winner is a tiny-data problem dressed up as a big one: there are 150 years of matches, but only one winner per tournament. I wanted to see which pre-tournament signals actually go with winning, without letting the tournament itself leak into the features.

## Decisions & tradeoffs

- **A star-schema warehouse.** Match results from 1872 to 2024 and FIFA rankings go into SQLite: a match fact table with team, tournament and date dimensions.
- **Six features, from the two years before each tournament:** win rate, goals scored and conceded per game, FIFA rank, host status and confederation. Matches from the tournament itself are excluded to prevent leakage.
- **A temporal split, not a random one.** Train on 1998–2018, validate on 2022 (won by Argentina).
- **One winner per ~32 teams** is heavy class imbalance, handled with balanced class weights. I compared Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Naive Bayes, Random Forest and XGBoost on accuracy, precision, recall and F1. Logistic Regression generalised best.

## What's next

With one positive example per tournament, any single prediction is fragile. Next I'd add Elo ratings and squad-level data, and evaluate with leave-one-tournament-out across every World Cup instead of one validation year.
